# Signal Forge channel strategy v3

Status: governing audience, editorial, and business strategy for the autonomous
studio. It extends the v2 creative specification without weakening its truth,
rights, quality, or publishing gates.

## 1. The channel is broad; the viewer promise is narrow

Signal Forge may cover any technology: AI, machine learning, cybersecurity,
software, hardware, robotics, consumer devices, the internet, gaming systems,
spaceflight, transportation, energy, biotech, digital money, retro computing,
and infrastructure.

The unifying promise is not “technology education.” It is:

> The wildest true stories hiding inside the machines shaping our lives.

Every video must deliver at least one of these emotions before explanation:
danger, awe, disbelief, ambition, conflict, mystery, status, absurdity, or
relief. A topic does not qualify merely because it is important or technical.

## 2. Audience job

The viewer is not opening Shorts to take a class. They want to witness something
impossible, dangerous, funny, hidden, or consequential. Signal Forge earns the
next second by making the situation change. Technical depth becomes the payoff
or the mechanism, never homework assigned before the story begins.

Long-form serves a different job: turn the curiosity earned by a Short into a
satisfying investigation, experiment, or systems story. It must still entertain;
it simply has room to explain.

## 3. Internal show portfolio

These are editorial lanes, not mandatory titles or visible branding.

| Lane | Emotional engine | Required proof | Natural long-form bridge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tech catastrophe | One normal action causes system-scale damage | incident receipt, consequence, causal chain | full failure reconstruction |
| Impossible build | A real artifact works under absurd constraints | working artifact, limits, measured result | build/test documentary |
| Cyber crime scene | A harmless-looking step opens an attack chain | advisory, affected system, defensive diagram | defensive incident case file |
| Hidden machine | An overlooked component controls something enormous | physical/digital artifact and dependency map | infrastructure deep dive |
| AI versus reality | A model succeeds or fails in a surprising way | demo, benchmark boundary, original test | reproducible experiment |
| Internet archaeology | A strange surviving system reveals forgotten history | archive, interface, timeline, modern consequence | historical systems story |
| Tech power struggle | Companies, governments, hackers, or communities fight over a system | primary statements and concrete stakes | multi-source investigation |
| Future arrives early | A research result becomes visibly real | paper, lab artifact, limitations, demonstration | mechanism and impact analysis |
| Expensive mistake | A tiny technical decision burns time, money, or trust | number, receipt, before/after state | engineering postmortem |
| Reddit confession | A first-person technical story escalates to a closed outcome | permissioned post receipt and attribution | only when independently sourceable |

The format router chooses the lane present in the source. It never forces a weak
story into a popular template.

## 4. Two-speed editorial desk

### Fast desk: consequential tech now

Poll authoritative feeds and approved communities for new incidents, launches,
research demonstrations, outages, exploits, product failures, court/regulatory
actions, and platform changes. Rank candidates by freshness, stakes, surprise,
visual evidence, source quality, and whether a closed payoff already exists.

Fast does not mean speculative. A breaking item is held when the central claim
has only one weak source, the outcome is still unknown, visuals cannot be used
honestly, or the first useful version would be a headline recap.

### Premium desk: durable stories

Continuously develop evergreen investigations, weird systems, impossible builds,
internet history, technical disasters, and original experiments. These provide
the visual ambition and long shelf life that breaking news often lacks.

Target weekly mix after the production system passes its gates:

- 40% timely stories;
- 30% durable case files and mysteries;
- 20% builds, experiments, and simulations;
- 10% permissioned Reddit stories.

This is an initial portfolio hypothesis, not a permanent quota. Analytics may
change it only after comparable samples exist.

## 5. The episode must have something to say

Before scripting, write a research dossier with:

1. one-sentence thesis;
2. the opening contradiction, consequence, or artifact;
3. three to five source-backed talking points that form a causal progression;
4. the strongest skeptical objection or limitation;
5. the closed result or honest current state;
6. why this treatment is distinct from reference videos;
7. a claim-to-source graph;
8. a claim-to-visual graph;
9. prohibited exaggerations and unknowns.

Every talking point must change the viewer's understanding. Definitions,
background, and jargon that do not change the state are cut. If the dossier has
no thesis, no turns, or no payoff, the source is not produced.

## 6. Shorts programming

Public target after the v3 private pilot: four to six passing Shorts per week.
There is no daily-upload obligation. A failed quality gate creates an empty slot,
not a lower-quality upload.

Each Short:

- stands alone and fully pays off its promise;
- uses 30–55 seconds only when the story supports that duration;
- introduces a meaningful narrative or visual change every few seconds;
- contains source-specific proof and an episode-specific visual idea;
- tests one declared variable against a valid baseline;
- may point to a related long-form video only after delivering its own result.

Shorts packaging optimizes for feed appeal: the first frame and first spoken
line are the package. Titles remain accurate and compact. Tags are metadata
hygiene, not a growth strategy.

## 7. Long-form programming

Start with one 8–16 minute video every two weeks. Increase only after two
consecutive episodes pass quality and resource gates without delaying Shorts.

Three launch formats:

- **Case File:** reconstruct a failure, attack, decision, or hidden dependency.
- **Build/Test:** reproduce a claim, benchmark a model, or build under a strange
  constraint.
- **System Story:** show how a technology became powerful, weird, fragile, or
  unavoidable.

Each long-form episode requires a cold open, thesis, act-level escalation,
evidence, a counterargument or limitation, original visual explanations, and a
resolved ending. Chapters describe meaningful acts, not arbitrary timestamps.

## 8. Performance model

YouTube states that Shorts ranking uses whether viewers choose to watch,
average view duration, average percentage viewed, and satisfaction signals. The
studio therefore optimizes four observable layers:

1. **Appeal:** viewed versus swiped away; long-form impressions and click-through.
2. **Engagement:** retention curve, average view duration, percentage viewed,
   rewatches, and completion.
3. **Satisfaction:** likes, comments with substance, shares, saves where
   available, subscribers gained, and negative feedback.
4. **Return:** returning viewers, follow-on episode starts, and long-form session
   depth.

Judge a video against matched channel baselines, not internet folklore. Every
weekly decision records sample size, uncertainty, and whether the treatment was
actually different. One weak upload does not trigger a channel-wide pivot.

## 9. Packaging and discovery

For each long-form episode generate at least five title concepts and three
thumbnail concepts before rendering the final package. Each concept maps to a
different accurate curiosity frame: consequence, contradiction, hidden cause,
impossible constraint, or result.

The autonomous packaging critic rejects:

- titles that repeat the thumbnail;
- keyword stuffing;
- claims stronger than the episode;
- thumbnails requiring small text;
- generic faces, arrows, glowing brains, fake code, or unexplained red circles;
- a package that attracts an audience the video cannot satisfy.

Search-oriented evergreen videos use the natural language viewers use for the
problem. Browse-oriented stories lead with stakes and novelty. Both must match
the video. YouTube's own guidance treats title, thumbnail, description, and
video content as relevance inputs while noting that tags are not essential.

## 10. Revenue architecture

Revenue is a portfolio, not a reason to corrupt topic selection.

### Stage A — prove audience value

Build repeat viewers and a catalog of original, rights-cleared work. Do not add
sponsor reads, affiliate pitches, or products before the channel has a stable
viewer promise and measured winning formats.

### Stage B — platform monetization

Maintain a policy-refresh task because YouTube eligibility and terms change.
Track public qualified Shorts views, public long-form watch hours, subscribers,
and channel-policy readiness separately. Private drafts do not count toward the
public thresholds.

### Stage C — aligned commercial layers

- Watch-page and Shorts revenue plus YouTube Premium revenue;
- fan funding when eligible and when the audience relationship supports it;
- tightly matched affiliate links for products actually demonstrated or tested;
- sponsors whose products survive the same claim and conflict checks as stories;
- premium research briefs, visual explainers, templates, or project files only
  after viewers repeatedly ask for deeper material;
- a newsletter/site archive that owns the source index and extends long-form
  investigations.

Shorts create discovery. Long-form builds trust and watch time. Useful owned
assets and carefully selected sponsors create margin. No revenue stream may buy
a positive conclusion or conceal a relationship.

## 12. Strategic non-goals

- mass-produced recaps;
- copying a competitor's script, sequence, branding, or footage;
- fake engagement, bought traffic, or warm-up manipulation;
- random broad topics with no shared viewer promise;
- endless tool demos with no conflict or result;
- optimizing tags while the idea, first frame, or story is weak;
- accepting AI-generated motion as a substitute for editorial judgment;
- publishing because the calendar has a hole.
